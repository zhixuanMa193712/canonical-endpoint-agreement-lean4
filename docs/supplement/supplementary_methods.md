The number 23 is the alternative budget inherited from the pre-outcome protocol; together with authored replay it gives 24 root-local executions. It was not selected by optimizing a witness rate, candidate-ablation result, or downstream outcome and has no claim to statistical or computational optimality. `Class` and `group` are frozen candidate-view ablation metadata; they are not execution semantics. The family label, assigned by lexical head, is the descriptive label used for witnesses spanning tactic families. Because authored tactics almost always take arguments, every realized panel consisted of entries 1--23, with entry 24 substituted only when the authored tactic equaled an earlier entry; entries 25--32 were never executed in any study reported in the paper. ### S3.1. Lexical head contract and external-label correction

The finite-library head rule strips surrounding whitespace and takes the first token before whitespace or `[`, `(`, `{`, `|`, or `;`. Question marks and underscores are retained. This is a lexical classifier, not a Lean AST parser or macro expander; inner commands are not substituted for the outer control head. Under this rule the 24 executed programs have 24 distinct heads, so on every executed panel the different-head condition is equivalent to requiring two distinct programs.

| Program | Lexical head | Genealogy treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `simp` / `simpa` / `simp_all` | `simp` / `simpa` / `simp_all` | Shared simplification engine |
| `aesop` / `aesop?` | `aesop` / `aesop?` | Shared Aesop engine; suggestion excluded in deduplicated view |
| `all_goals simp` | `all_goals` | Direct control wrapper; simplification genealogy |
| `all_goals aesop` | `all_goals` | Direct control wrapper; Aesop genealogy |
| `first \| rfl \| assumption` | `first` | Direct control wrapper |

The external-study implementation contained an over-escaped regular-expression character class: it split on literal `s` instead of whitespace, mapping `simp`, `simpa`, `simp_all`, and `solve_by_elim` to `<empty>` and both Aesop forms to `ae`, and truncating `assumption`, `constructor`, `positivity`, and `apply_assumption` at their first `s`, which left those four heads distinct. The v1.21 correction derives heads and descriptive families from the stored tactic text, then recomputes cross-head witnesses using the unchanged structural endpoint hashes. The four-repository and multi-step protocol functions use the correctly escaped head rule. External genealogy selection uses exact full-program dictionary entries and is unchanged. Pairs among these programs were therefore wrongly excluded as same-head pairs, which lowered the archived full-panel counts. The original reconstruction audit used the same head function and did not detect the defect. The genealogy deduplication itself selects programs by exact text, but witnesses between programs in the deduplicated view, such as `simp` and `solve_by_elim`, were also affected; that the 141 deduplicated positive roots are the same before and after the correction is an empirical result, checked root by root, and the deduplicated pair counts in Section S20 use the corrected heads. The continuation study (Exp52) selected its witness roots from the initial frame with the defective labels; recomputed with corrected heads, its eligible set is unchanged (40 roots in seven repositories). The depth-two study (Exp54) used case-series rows, whose head rule was correct. This is a disclosed post-outcome analysis correction, not a new prospective analysis or an execution rerun. Sections S11 and S20 report corrected external results. Source ZIPs and their hashes remain unchanged; the correction code, root rows, and summaries form an additional reproducibility layer that must accompany the public release.

## S5. Live execution pipeline

1. Verify repository, commit, toolchain, source, configuration, and code identities. The configuration index `xi` includes Lean/toolchain version, repository commit and imports, options, heartbeat/timeout limits, process-isolation policy, runner/parser version, and canonicalizer version.
2. Elaborate unchanged source and locate exactly one frozen `TacticInfo` occurrence; when nested nodes share the frozen span, select the node whose parent is a tactic sequence (`tacticSeq1Indented`, `tacticSeqBracketed`, or `rewriteSeq`).
3. Recover `ContextInfo`, `mctxBefore`, and ordered `goalsBefore`; require one goal.
4. Replay exact authored syntax with the candidate executor, under the same execution limits as the candidates, and require its canonical records to equal those of the recorded state after the tactic (`mctxAfter`, `goalsAfter`): the textual record in the case series, and the textual and structural records in the later studies.
5. Start every candidate from the same recovered pre-state in an independent `MetaM` branch.
6. Retain failure or serialize every prespecified endpoint.
7. Require complete candidate accounting before pair construction.
8. Derive root and repository summaries from immutable rows.
9. Independently reconstruct prespecified summaries and verify export manifests.

### Execution semantics and resource limits

Each candidate is parsed and run by `Lean.Elab.Tactic.run` on the unique starting goal inside `Term.TermElabM.run` with an empty `Term.Context` and `Term.State`, within `ContextInfo.runMetaM` on the recorded `ContextInfo` and `mctxBefore`. Every call to `runMetaM` constructs a `CoreM` context with Lean's default limits and then applies the options recorded in the `ContextInfo`. Because the heartbeat limit is fixed when the context is created, each branch has Lean's default of 200,000 heartbeats regardless of `set_option maxHeartbeats` in the source file, as the archived diagnostics of heartbeat failures report; the other recorded options, including `maxRecDepth`, apply. Each branch receives a fresh heartbeat budget, whereas the authored tactic shared the budget of its declaration. `Tactic.run` is not followed by a call that forces pending synthetic metavariables. All branches of a root run in one `lake env lean --run` process. The archived runners' default wall-clock limits for that process were 900 s in the case-series and sensitivity runs and 1,200 s in the strict frame, and every root process completed. Heartbeat exhaustion caused one case-series failure (`solve_by_elim`) and 23 strict-frame failures at six roots; all are recorded as tactic failures. The strict-frame heartbeat failures were `rfl`, `trivial`, `aesop`, `aesop?`, `constructor`, and `solve_by_elim` at three `poseidon2-fv` roots, `aesop` and `aesop?` at one root each of `wowii144-lean` and `lean4-search-algorithms`, and `solve_by_elim` at one `GlimpseOfLean` root. A post hoc scan of the source files (`scan_options.py`) found `set_option` commands in scope at no case-series root and at 61 strict-frame roots, mostly linter options; nine strict-frame roots have a raised `maxHeartbeats` in scope, and none of the seven roots with heartbeat failures does. Options set in Lake configurations or inside proofs were not scanned.

A branch succeeds when parsing and execution raise no exception. `Tactic.run` catches the exception that aborts a tactic block and returns the remaining goals, and the default tactic context enables error recovery, under which some tactics log an error instead of throwing one. Messages logged during the branch are not recorded, and the resulting metavariable assignment is not inspected for `sorryAx`. No-progress behavior differs between programs: `simp`, `simp_all`, and `ring_nf` fail when they change nothing, whereas current Mathlib's `elabNormNum` calls its location transformer with `ifUnchanged := .silent`, so `norm_num` can succeed without changing the goal. In the strict frame `norm_num` succeeded at all 278 roots where it parsed. With the default term context some elaboration errors are converted to `sorry`; the candidate programs take no term arguments, but this route is not excluded.

Lean's command elaborator stores the environment after the command in each information tree, so the recorded `ContextInfo` carries an environment that contains the declaration being proved and any attributes it registers. Candidate executions can therefore use the theorem itself, for example through library search. Authored replay compares goal and metavariable records and does not check the environment. In the multistep study, LeanDojo runs tactics inside the theorem's own proof, before the theorem enters the environment. Proof terms were not stored, so such uses cannot be identified individually. Because nearly all roots start a theorem's proof, this matters: `exact?` closed 92 of 93 and 437 of 474 goals at such roots, against 1 of 3 and 14 of 38 elsewhere (Section S8.1). Removing `exact?` changes 2 of the 57 case-series positives (textual) and 2 of the 355 strict-frame positives (structural), because other programs also close those goals. Section S8.2 reports a conservative recount that also drops every root whose declaration carries a `simp`, `ext`, `fun_prop`, `aesop`, `norm_num`, `positivity`, or `grind` attribute (15 and 18 roots). The attribute scan reads `@[...]` blocks before the declaration, including attributes given as `(attr := ...)`, for example `@[to_additive (attr := simp)]`, and `attribute [...]` commands naming the declaration.

## S6. Endpoint content

`raw` is the ordered goal display produced by the worker's printer, which mirrors Lean's goal view: local contexts are printed after `sanitizeNames`, so inaccessible names appear with a cross mark (U+271D, often described as a dagger) and a superscript index; case tags are printed; and default pretty-printer options apply, which hide implicit and instance arguments and abbreviate proof subterms as `⋯`. Universe metavariables are not renamed by `text48`. `text48` replaces matches of `(?:(?:\?m\.|_uniq\.|_let_match\.|_match\.)\d+|<mark>\d*)`, where `<mark>` is U+271D, by `<GEN>`, strips each line, collapses horizontal whitespace, and drops blank lines. Because `\d` does not match superscript digits, the index after the mark is retained (for example `inst<GEN>²`). Raw and textual classifications coincided in every analysis.

The strict structural schema `exp49-strict-goal-structure-v1` serializes, for each goal in order, the metavariable kind and scope depth, the local-instance registry, every local declaration in context order (declaration kind, binder information, local-instance membership, type, and let value with nondependency flag), and the target expression. Free variables are named by their position in the local context; user-facing names and case tags are not serialized. Assigned expression and level metavariables are replaced by their assignments, delayed assignments are not followed, unassigned metavariables and level parameters are numbered by first occurrence across all goals of the state, binder names inside expressions are not serialized, and metadata nodes are stripped. Auxiliary local declarations are included. Instance arguments are serialized like any other subterm, so a tactic that rewrites an instance path changes the structural record even when the printed state is unchanged (Section S9). Both canonicalizers depend on goal order. Proof terms, timing, messages, and internal tactic traces are excluded. Because hypothesis names are omitted, two states that differ only in names match structurally, although a later tactic that refers to a hypothesis by name could treat them differently. The same-root disagreement table demonstrates that these definitions are related but not identical.

### Same-server comparison fields

Re-execution comparisons use branch status, ordered raw state bytes, goal count, terminality, and the prespecified canonical endpoints. Runner-specific container schema labels are metadata rather than endpoint content. The final comparison checks these content fields without changing the underlying execution rows; its complete mode-by-mode results appear in Section S10.

## S7. Statistical reporting policy

- Case-series and candidate-sensitivity intervals are archived repository-bootstrap percentile outputs over four observed repository rates. The manuscript calls them descriptive repository-resampling intervals. Such an interval can take at most 35 distinct values; for the case-series rates a t-interval with three degrees of freedom is [41.1%, 77.6%], against the prespecified [50.00%, 68.75%].
- Initial external-frame intervals come from the archived two-level repository-to-root bootstrap over a screened frozen frame. They are not ecosystem-level intervals.
- The bounded-continuation study's `[1,1]` concordance interval and empirical one-sided discordance value `0` result from resampling an all-concordant observed witness sample. They are not calibrated unseen-event bounds.
- The external hard-root study's process-call interval is a 20,000-replicate dataset-stratified percentile bootstrap over 64 frozen roots, with 32 roots from each of miniF2F and PutnamBench. Its separately frozen one-sided lower bound is the normal approximation `mean(d) - z_0.95 * sd(d) / sqrt(n)`. Efficiency uses all launched calls on all roots. Coverage is not imputed for the one operationally unevaluable root; the 63-root Wilson upper bound is complete-case sensitivity analysis only.
- The oracle-assisted multi-step study's coverage loss fraction counts every operationally incomplete pair as a loss and uses a one-sided 95% Wilson score upper bound. The Wilson value is an uncertainty summary under a binomial working model, not a population bound for mathlib or the Lean ecosystem. The exact one-sided Clopper--Pearson bound for 0/64 is 0.0457; for the 32 roots of authored depth 3--6 the Wilson and Clopper--Pearson bounds for 0/32 are 0.0780 and 0.0894. Endpoint selection altered the search only at the 13 roots where it expanded fewer nodes and at the five roots that neither policy solved; a loss was possible only at the 12 of the 13 that full expansion solved, and for 0/12 the two bounds are 0.184 and 0.221. Because losses are excluded under deterministic execution and an adequate key, the frozen coverage criterion amounted to observing no loss on at least 52 complete pairs. Its efficiency estimand is the paired full-minus-endpoint tactic-invocation count over 64 roots. The two-sided interval is a 20,000-replicate authored-depth-stratified percentile bootstrap; the separately frozen one-sided lower bound is the normal approximation `mean(d) - z_0.95 * sd(d) / sqrt(n)`.
- The strict external-frame study's primary estimand is the equal-weight mean of the 32 repository-specific full-panel root frequencies. Its interval is a two-level nonparametric bootstrap that resamples the observed repository representatives and then their roots for 20,000 replicates with PCG64 seed `20260918` in the corrected analysis. The genealogy-deduplicated sensitivity estimate uses the same resampling design. These are descriptive empirical frame-resampling stability summaries, not population intervals; the procedure treats the observed representatives and within-repository roots as empirical resampling units and does not turn the lineage heuristic into a proof of independence. The archive also reports a source-file sensitivity: within each repository it averages the sampled-file-specific root frequencies equally, then averages the 32 repository values equally. Because all 512 roots were evaluable, complete-case estimates and frozen-frame worst-case missingness bounds coincide. Resampling only representatives (20,000 replicates, seed `20260918`) gives [59.8%, 78.5%] for the full panel and [21.9%, 33.8%] after deduplication. A post hoc frame-conditional summary holds the 32 representatives fixed and resamples only roots within each (20,000 replicates, seed `20260918`): [66.2%, 72.5%] for the full panel and [24.0%, 31.3%] after deduplication.
- The post hoc size controls in Section S8.3 use 1,000 random panels per setting: 18 programs drawn from each root's panel (seed 18), or the panel without five programs drawn from those that succeeded at least once in the sample (seed 5). They are descriptive.
- The depth-stratified invocation and saving distributions reported in the revision are exact tabulations of immutable multi-step job receipts; no post hoc inferential subgroup test was introduced. Candidate-view/canonicalizer and terminality tables are likewise exact tabulations of archived root classifications.
- Historical planning thresholds and archived protocol labels are retained for exact reconstruction. Because their substantive scientific basis was not separately documented, the main text interprets point estimates, uncertainty summaries, and realized support directly rather than treating those targets as universal validity boundaries.

### Formal controls used in the main text

Neither frozen canonicalizer refines the other: text-only and structure-only matches both occur on the measured rows (main Section 4.5 and Section S9), so their endpoint relations are not nested.

For a fixed root, execution configuration, and canonicalizer, enlarging the candidate panel cannot remove an already observed witness, as stated in main Section 2.3. The root indicator depends on the candidates available at that root. This implication is one-way: a zero under a finite panel does not imply absence among all Lean programs.

### Frozen downstream root, pair, and representative selection

The downstream intervention uses no probe outcome during selection. A root is eligible only if its archived robustness row is evaluable, has one pre-goal, and contains both a non-initial nonterminal cross-head equal-strict-endpoint pair and a corresponding different-endpoint control pair. Archive members above 20 MiB are excluded by a frozen portability gate. Eligible roots are ordered by archive-member bytes and theorem identifier, and the first four per repository are selected.

Within each root, branches are ordered by frozen candidate position. Eligible pair records are ordered by their strict-endpoint hash tuple and then by the two frozen candidate positions. The first witness and first control are retained. The witness representative is the left branch in this frozen order. The rule depends only on archived depth-1 identities and positions; downstream probe outcomes cannot select or replace a root, pair, or representative.

### External hard-root selection and four-policy execution

Pinned miniF2F commit `f0a20e14c1eeccd859d51bb4c2b3ee487889c303` and PutnamBench commit `34eba93650f2fa803ca6aae6dfd2c9f22d46c00d` were built under Lean v4.27.0. Complete source enumeration identified theorem holes, and candidate-only screening used 21 frozen expansion tactics: `rfl`, `assumption`, `trivial`, `contradiction`, `simp`, `simpa`, `simp_all`, `aesop`, `norm_num`, `omega`, `linarith`, `nlinarith`, `ring`, `ring_nf`, `positivity`, `decide`, `native_decide`, `constructor`, `ext`, `fun_prop`, and `solve_by_elim`. Suggestion queries `exact?` and `aesop?` were excluded before screening. Of 658 screened roots, 459 were eligible, 28 failed the minimum effect-class size, 100 failed the minimum executable-nonterminal count, and 71 had a terminal candidate.

Every root retained an eligibility receipt, source partition, deterministic pilot key, deterministic confirmation key, selection rank, and exclusion reason. The pilot selected 16 roots per dataset. A 20,000-replicate stratified simulation then fixed a disjoint confirmation of 32 roots per dataset before any confirmation continuation probe. The selected-root ledger SHA-256 is `cbfc4569e5869e74b8f72e4ee5f4ce6f1def6d23fdde274ef15e224a169d6bb6`; the complete selection-audit SHA-256 is `2c341eeed3b5e8729f2861cac867918c53faa2bff2fbe66717510c8f631b745b`.

All four policies execute the same ordered 21-candidate front end. Full expansion follows every successful nonterminal branch. Endpoint selection keeps the first branch for each structural endpoint hash. Deduplication by tactic head keeps the first branch for each parsed head. The random control matching child counts at each node (archive label `fixed_budget_random`) uses a root-specific frozen seed to select exactly as many branches as endpoint selection. Each retained branch receives the same ordered eight-probe panel. Candidate calls and branch--probe calls each launch fresh Lean processes; cell timeout is 900 seconds and the common root--policy budget is 7,200 seconds.

The primary efficiency estimand is the paired per-root difference in actual Lean process calls between full and endpoint policies. The archived coverage estimand is the fraction of roots solved by full but not endpoint selection, with an inherited planning margin of 0.05. Endpoint-union and per-root endpoint-set equality are diagnostics. One root has an unavailable coverage outcome because three policy cells encountered a Lean internal panic. All launched processes remain counted for efficiency; coverage is not imputed. The planning margin is retained in the protocol for reconstruction and is not treated as a universal scientific threshold.

### Oracle-assisted known-solvable multi-step selection and four-policy execution

The oracle-assisted multi-step study uses the 2,000 theorem rows in LeanDojo benchmark partition `leandojo_benchmark_4/random/test.json` at mathlib4 commit `29dcec074de168ac2bf835a77ef68bbe069194c5`. Offline graph analysis admitted 409 roots whose shortest recorded authored path had depth 2--6 and contained neither `sorry` nor `admit`. A role- and depth-stratified 192-root candidate frame was frozen before any scientific policy job. The mathlib4 commit pins the toolchain `leanprover/lean4:v4.10.0-rc1`, and LeanDojo 4.20.0 provided the interaction. Live qualification completed all 192 receipts: 142 passed and 50 failed (25 mismatches of the initial state, where LeanDojo's live initial state did not reproduce the state recorded in the benchmark, 17 roots closable in one step by a panel program, seven authored-replay failures, and one operational failure). The qualified pool for the main run contained 38 depth-2 roots and 55 depth-3--6 roots. The main run selected the first 32 frozen-rank passing roots in each stratum, disjoint from the 16-root pilot. Its deeper stratum contains 20, 8, 1, and 3 roots of authored depth 3, 4, 5, and 6, and the 64 roots come from 63 source files. The total of 64 was frozen before main-run outcomes as a balanced computational budget of 32 roots per authored-depth stratum; the archived protocol contains no power or precision derivation for this number.

The generic candidate panel contains `rfl`, `assumption`, `solve_by_elim`, `trivial`, `contradiction`, `simp`, `simp only`, `simp_all`, `simpa`, two fixed-budget `aesop` variants, `norm_num`, `omega`, `linarith`, `nlinarith`, `ring`, `ring_nf`, `positivity`, `constructor`, `constructor <;> try assumption`, `intro`, `intro h`, `ext`, and `fun_prop`. Each root's authored steps other than `sorry` and `admit` are added, giving 25--30 programs, and the union is sorted by a root-specific hash (seed `620052`), so the generic programs do not appear in the order listed. All authored actions remain in the panel at every node. An authored action is recognized at a node when the node's `text48_v1` key equals the key recorded for the authored state that precedes it; it is then moved to index 3, or rank four. The main text calls these actions supplied authored steps and reserves the word witness for qualifying pairs. Every policy is therefore offered the same disclosed route to a proof, although deduplication by tactic head and the random control can discard the child it produces; this intentionally neutralizes candidate acquisition. Every main-run panel contained all of the root's authored steps.

Search runs through fresh LeanDojo theorem sessions. Full expansion retains all successful children. Endpoint selection (archive label `effect_aware`) retains the first child per normalized `text48_v1` endpoint key. This key is the SHA-256 digest of LeanDojo's `TacticState.pp` after CRLF/CR-to-LF conversion, removal of surrounding and blank-line whitespace, per-line collapse of horizontal whitespace, and replacement of generated identifiers by `<GEN>`. It reuses the string-normalization logic of the primary textual canonicalizer but operates on the LeanDojo pretty-printed state; it is not the structural serializer and does not preserve the complete ordered local context or metavariable declarations. The name records the original archive identifier and schema version, not 48 state fields, and its implementation was frozen before main-run outcomes. Deduplication by tactic head (archive label `head_dedup`) retains the first child per parsed tactic head. The random control matching child counts at each node (archive label `fixed_budget_random`) uses seed `620054` to retain the same child count as endpoint selection at each visited node. Root-specific policy order is a deterministic cyclic Latin order. Policies execute sequentially within root. The depth, node, invocation, and time limits are recorded in the protocol frozen before the pilot, which also records that budgets were not tuned after it. Search is breadth-first over a first-in, first-out queue and stops at the first `ProofFinished` result returned by LeanDojo; found proofs were not recompiled from source. A child whose endpoint key matches that of the expanded node or any of its ancestors is discarded as a cycle, and no policy merges repeated states reached through different branches. The common scientific limits are depth 6, 32 expanded nodes, 768 tactic invocations, and 240 seconds beginning only after live initial-state validation. All four policies execute every candidate at each expanded node and differ only in which children they enqueue. The invocation counter increases before each candidate is executed, so every executed candidate is counted, whether or not its child is retained. Repository/theorem initialization has a separate 1,800-second envelope and finalization a 120-second grace period.

The 16-root pilot completed all policy jobs and produced 13 full-policy multi-step solves before the main run was frozen. The main run comprised 64 roots and 256 jobs.

### Post hoc replay of the search logs

The archived transition logs record, for every expanded node, its endpoint key and, for each executed candidate, the outcome kind and the child's key. The replay (`round2_analysis.py`) maps each key to the first complete expansion recorded for it and reruns breadth-first search with the archived limits in three modes: full expansion, sibling-only endpoint selection, and a global set of seen keys that discards any child whose key was enqueued anywhere earlier in the search. It assumes deterministic execution and a key that determines all outcomes at a node, so it measures cost and cannot detect losses caused by merging. Full expansion and sibling-only selection are replayed from the full-expansion logs as a validation; the global mode uses the logs of all four policies and is reported only for roots where every node it needs was logged. Two further replays (`round3_analysis.py`) attribute the invocations that full expansion spent in subtrees rooted at a duplicate sibling to the pair of programs that produced the duplicate, and rerun full expansion and sibling-only selection with `ring_nf`, the Aesop configuration with `maxRuleApplications := 16`, and `constructor <;> try assumption` removed from the generic panel (unless they are authored steps), moving a recognized supplied step back to rank four. The coverage estimand is the fraction of roots solved by full but not endpoint selection, counting every operationally incomplete pair as a loss. Historical planning cutoffs remain in the protocol; the article interprets the observed loss fraction, uncertainty summary, saving distribution, and depth strata directly. Seeds `620051`--`620055` fix the frame, candidate order, policy order, random control, and bootstrap respectively.
